## 1. Purpose

Nexora Technologies Pvt. Ltd. ("Nexora", "the Company") depends on the trust of its customers, its colleagues, its investors, and the communities in which it operates. This Code of Conduct and Ethics Policy describes the standards of behaviour that every person working for or on behalf of Nexora is expected to meet, and explains how to raise concerns safely.

The Code applies alongside the other Nexora policies. In particular, it works with the Human Resources Policy Manual (NXR-HR-001), which governs disciplinary procedures and grievances; the Information Technology Security Policy (NXR-IT-001), which governs the protection of information and systems; the Remote Work and Hybrid Working Policy (NXR-HR-002); the Travel and Expense Reimbursement Policy (NXR-FIN-001); and the Engineering Guidelines and Operations Handbook (NXR-ENG-001).

## 2. Scope

2.1 This Code applies to all directors, officers, employees (including those on probation), fixed-term and part-time staff, interns, apprentices, contractors, consultants, and agents of Nexora, at all locations including the Pune headquarters, the Bengaluru office, and the Hyderabad office, and when working remotely or travelling on Company business.

2.2 It applies during working hours, at Company events, on Company premises, on Company systems, and in any setting where a person's conduct is connected with Nexora, including online interactions and travel.

2.3 It applies to third parties, such as vendors and partners, through the terms of their contracts. Nexora expects them to uphold standards consistent with this Code.

## 3. Governance and Roles

3.1 **Ethics Committee.** The Ethics Committee oversees the application of this Code. Its members are the Head of Legal & Compliance (Chair), the CHRO, the CFO (Sanjay Iyer), the CISO (Rohan Kulkarni), and one independent external member appointed by the Audit Committee. The Ethics Committee meets quarterly and whenever a serious matter is referred to it.

3.2 **Internal Committee (IC).** The Internal Committee is constituted under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (the "POSH Act"). It is chaired by a Presiding Officer, Sunita Kapoor, who is a senior woman employee, and includes at least four members, half of whom are women, and one external member from a non-governmental organisation or a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment. Separate Internal Committees serve the Pune, Bengaluru, and Hyderabad offices.

3.3 **Head of Legal & Compliance.** The Head of Legal & Compliance owns this Code, advises on legal and ethical questions, keeps the gift and conflict registers, and receives reports made through the ethics helpline.

3.4 **Managers.** Managers are role models. They must set the tone, must not ignore or tolerate breaches of this Code, and must escalate concerns promptly to the HRBP or the Ethics Committee.

3.5 **Data Protection Officer (DPO).** The DPO, Nisha Patil, advises on privacy matters as described in Section 9.

3.6 **Everyone.** Every person covered by this Code is personally responsible for reading it, completing the Code of Conduct training within 10 working days of joining (as required by Section 4.5 of NXR-HR-001) and annually thereafter by the last working day of June, and for asking for advice when unsure.

## 4. Core Principles

4.1 **Integrity.** We are honest and transparent in our dealings. We do not mislead customers, colleagues, or regulators. We keep accurate records, and we do not falsify documents, timesheets, attendance records, expense claims, or test results.

4.2 **Respect and inclusion.** We treat everyone with dignity and respect, and do not tolerate discrimination on the grounds of gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, caste, religion, race, ethnicity, national origin, age, disability, marital status, or any other characteristic protected by law. Employment decisions, including recruitment, probation, confirmation, promotion, and termination, must be made on job-related criteria only, in line with Sections 4, 5, and 9 of NXR-HR-001.

4.3 **Compliance with law.** We comply with all applicable laws and regulations, including those relating to labour, taxation, data protection, anti-corruption, competition, export controls, and foreign exchange. If Nexora policy and the law differ, the stricter standard applies.

4.4 **Accountability.** We take responsibility for our decisions and for our mistakes, and we raise problems early. We do not blame others for our own failures. Honest mistakes reported promptly, including security mistakes reported under Section 9.2 of NXR-IT-001, are treated as opportunities to learn.

4.5 **Professionalism.** We communicate courteously, respect colleagues' time, and act as good representatives of Nexora in every interaction, whether in the office, online, or with customers. Working remotely under NXR-HR-002 does not change the standards that apply.

4.6 **Safe workplace.** We comply with health and safety rules. Working under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs, bringing weapons to the workplace, and threatening or violent behaviour are prohibited.

4.7 **Fair competition.** We compete honestly. We do not agree with competitors on prices, markets, or customers, and we do not obtain competitors' confidential information through improper means.

## 5. Prevention of Harassment and Sexual Harassment (POSH)

5.1 **Policy statement.** Nexora has zero tolerance for harassment, bullying, intimidation, and sexual harassment. All employees, including interns, contractors, and visitors, have the right to work in an environment that is free from such conduct.

5.2 **Sexual harassment.** Sexual harassment includes unwelcome acts or behaviour, whether direct or by implication, such as physical contact and advances; a demand or request for sexual favours; sexually coloured remarks; showing pornography; or any other unwelcome physical, verbal, or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature. It may occur in person, in the workplace, at off-site events, on travel, and online, including in messaging platforms and on video calls during remote working.

5.3 **Other harassment and bullying.** Harassment includes offensive jokes, slurs, insults, exclusion, humiliation, repeated unreasonable criticism, and threats. Managers must not misuse their authority, including during probation reviews or performance evaluations, to pressure or punish employees.

5.4 **Complaints under the POSH Act.** A woman who has experienced sexual harassment may file a written complaint with the Internal Committee within **3 months** of the incident (or the last incident in a series). The IC may extend this period by up to 3 more months if it is satisfied that circumstances prevented the complaint being filed. Complaints may be sent to the IC at ic@nexoratech.example. The IC will assist a person who cannot put the complaint in writing.

5.5 **Inquiry process.** The IC will send a copy of the complaint to the respondent within 7 working days, give both parties a fair opportunity to be heard, and complete the inquiry within **90 days**. The IC will submit its report to the Company within 10 days of completion. The Company will act on the recommendations within 60 days. Conciliation is available only at the complainant's request and never involves the payment of a monetary settlement as the basis of conciliation.

5.6 **Interim relief.** During the inquiry, the IC may recommend interim measures, such as transferring the complainant or the respondent, granting leave to the complainant of up to 3 months in addition to other leave, or restraining the respondent from reporting on the complainant's work.

5.7 **Confidentiality.** Proceedings are confidential. Identity of the complainant, respondent, witnesses, and details of the inquiry must not be disclosed except as required by law. Breaching confidentiality is a serious offence.

5.8 **Other harassment.** Complaints about harassment or discrimination that do not fall under the POSH Act may be raised under Section 12 of NXR-HR-001 or through the channels in Section 11 of this Code. Such matters are investigated by the HRBP or the Ethics Committee.

5.9 **No retaliation.** Retaliation against a person who has made a complaint or participated in an inquiry is prohibited and will be treated as gross misconduct.

5.10 **Awareness.** Nexora holds POSH awareness sessions at least twice a year, and all employees must complete the POSH module during onboarding, as required by Section 4.5 of NXR-HR-001. The IC's contact details are displayed on NexoraHub and in each office.

## 6. Conflicts of Interest

6.1 **Definition.** A conflict of interest arises when a personal interest, relationship, or outside activity influences, or could appear to influence, a person's judgment in the interests of Nexora.

6.2 **Examples.** Conflicts may arise from:

- A financial interest of more than 1 percent in a competitor, customer, or supplier of Nexora, or a role as a director or officer of such an organisation.
- Hiring, managing, or influencing the pay or promotion of a family member or a person with whom the employee has a close personal relationship.
- Awarding business to a company owned or controlled by a relative or friend.
- Using Company resources, time, or confidential information for a personal business.
- Taking up outside employment, consulting, or directorships.

6.3 **Declaration.** All employees must declare any actual or potential conflict of interest to their reporting manager and to the Head of Legal & Compliance as soon as it arises. In addition, all employees at grade M1 and above, all Finance and Procurement employees, and all employees with authority to approve vendors must complete an annual conflict of interest declaration by **30 April** each year.

6.4 **Outside employment.** Employees must not undertake outside paid work or business activities without written approval from their Department Head and the HRBP. Approval will not be given for work with competitors, or where the work would interfere with the employee's duties, or where there is a risk to Nexora's confidential information.

6.5 **Related-party relationships.** Relatives and close personal partners must not be in a direct reporting line to each other. Where a relationship develops, the parties must declare it, and the Company will arrange for the reporting line or decision-making authority to change.

6.6 **Management of conflicts.** The Head of Legal & Compliance will decide how a declared conflict is managed, for example by recusal from a decision, and will record the decision in the conflict register.

6.7 **Approvals.** No one may approve or process a transaction in which they have a conflict. This includes travel or expense claims: in line with Section 4.4 of NXR-FIN-001, nobody may approve their own expense claim or travel request.

## 7. Gifts, Hospitality, and Anti-Corruption

7.1 **Principle.** We win business on the merits of our products and services. We do not give or accept anything of value in order to influence a business decision, or that could appear to do so.

7.2 **Receiving gifts and hospitality.**

| Value (per giver, per year) | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹2,000 | May be accepted; no declaration needed if occasional and customary |
| Above ₹2,000 and up to ₹5,000 | May be accepted only if declared to the reporting manager and recorded in the gift register kept by Legal & Compliance |
| Above ₹5,000 | Must be declined or returned. If refusal would cause serious offence, the gift must be handed to Legal & Compliance, which will decide how it is used |

7.3 **Never accept** cash or cash equivalents, gifts offered during a tender or vendor selection, or anything that is offered in return for favours.

7.4 **Giving gifts and hospitality.** Gifts, meals, and entertainment offered to customers or business partners must be modest, transparent, legal, and infrequent. A gift or hospitality with a value above **₹5,000** per recipient per year must be approved by the Department Head and declared to Legal & Compliance before it is given, as noted in Section 12.2 of NXR-FIN-001. The costs must be recorded accurately in NexoraExpense.

7.5 **Public officials.** No gift, hospitality, or payment of any kind may be offered to a government or public official, either directly or through a third party, without the prior written approval of the Head of Legal & Compliance. Facilitation payments are prohibited.

7.6 **Anti-corruption.** Nexora prohibits bribery in all forms. No one may offer, give, request, or accept a bribe, directly or indirectly. The Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, and other applicable anti-bribery laws apply to Nexora and to its personnel.

7.7 **Charitable and political contributions.** Donations to charities or political parties in Nexora's name require the CEO's approval and the Head of Legal & Compliance's review. Employees must not use Company time or resources for personal political activities.

## 8. Confidentiality and Intellectual Property

8.1 **Confidential information.** Confidential information includes anything not publicly available that relates to Nexora, its customers, or its partners, such as source code, product roadmaps, architectures, customer lists and data, pricing, financial results, contracts, and personnel information. It is classified and protected according to Section 4 of NXR-IT-001.

8.2 **Duty of confidentiality.** Employees must not use or disclose confidential information except as required for their work or authorised in writing. The duty continues after leaving Nexora: for **2 years** after separation for general confidential business information, and indefinitely for trade secrets, source code, and customer data.

8.3 **Protecting information.** Confidential information must be handled in line with NXR-IT-001. Employees must not discuss confidential matters in public places, on social media, or with family and friends, and must use only approved systems and devices, including when working remotely under NXR-HR-002.

8.4 **Recording of meetings.** Meetings must not be recorded, and transcripts must not be created, without the consent of participants, as set out in Section 10.4 of NXR-HR-002. Recordings of meetings that involve Confidential or Restricted information must be stored in approved repositories only.

8.5 **Intellectual property.** All work created by employees in the course of their employment, including software, designs, inventions, documents, and data, belongs to Nexora as set out in the appointment letter. Employees must disclose and assign such work to Nexora on request.

8.6 **Prior work and third-party material.** Employees must not bring confidential information or intellectual property of previous employers or other third parties to Nexora, and must not use third-party materials without the right to do so. Open-source components must be used in line with Section 13 of NXR-ENG-001.

8.7 **Insider information.** Employees who learn of material non-public information about Nexora or its customers or partners must not use it for personal gain or share it with others.

8.8 **Social media and public statements.** Only authorised spokespersons may speak on behalf of Nexora. Employees using social media in a personal capacity must make clear that the views are their own, and must not disclose confidential information or make derogatory comments about colleagues, customers, or competitors.

## 9. Data Privacy and Responsible Use of Data

9.1 **Commitment.** Nexora handles personal data lawfully, fairly, and transparently, in accordance with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) and other applicable laws. Personal data of customers, employees, candidates, and partners is classified Restricted under Section 4 of NXR-IT-001.

9.2 **Principles.** Personal data must be:

- collected only for specified and lawful purposes and used only for those purposes;
- limited to what is necessary;
- accurate and kept up to date;
- retained only for as long as necessary;
- protected by appropriate security controls; and
- accessed only by people who need it to do their jobs.

9.3 **Access restrictions.** No one may look at personal data, whether of customers, employees, or anybody else, out of curiosity or for a non-work reason. Access to personal data in production systems must follow the strict controls in Section 6 of NXR-IT-001 and Section 8 of NXR-ENG-001, and is monitored.

9.4 **Employee data.** Employee personal data held on NexoraPeople is Restricted and is accessed only in line with Section 13 of NXR-HR-001. Monitoring of Company systems is carried out for security and compliance purposes, in line with Section 11 of NXR-IT-001, and is proportionate and transparent.

9.5 **Data breaches.** Anyone who becomes aware of an actual or suspected breach of personal data must report it to security@nexoratech.example **within one hour**, as required by Section 9.2 of NXR-IT-001. The DPO, the CISO, and the Head of Legal & Compliance will decide about notification to the Data Protection Board of India and to affected individuals. Nobody else may notify regulators or individuals.

9.6 **Data subject rights.** Requests from individuals to access, correct, or delete their personal data must be forwarded immediately to the DPO at dpo@nexoratech.example. Employees must not respond to such requests themselves.

9.7 **Privacy by design.** New products, features, and vendors that process personal data must be reviewed by the DPO's team before launch or contract signature, in line with Section 12.5 of NXR-ENG-001 and Section 10.3 of NXR-IT-001.

## 10. Speaking Up and Whistleblower Protection

10.1 **Duty and right to speak up.** Everyone covered by this Code is encouraged, and employees at grade M1 and above are expected, to report promptly any conduct that they reasonably believe breaches this Code, the law, or any Nexora policy. Examples include fraud, bribery, falsified expenses or records, harassment, data breaches, security violations, and unsafe practices.

10.2 **Good faith.** Reports must be made in good faith. A person who makes a report honestly, even if it turns out to be mistaken, will be protected. Knowingly false or malicious reports are a breach of this Code.

10.3 **Anonymity and confidentiality.** Reports may be made anonymously through the independent, third-party ethics hotline. Nexora will keep the identity of the reporter confidential to the extent possible and permitted by law, and will share information only with those who need it to investigate.

10.4 **Protection from retaliation.** Nexora prohibits retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith or cooperates in an investigation. Retaliation includes dismissal, demotion, denial of promotion, unfavourable performance ratings, denial of confirmation for an employee on probation, changes to work arrangements (including withdrawal of approved remote work under NXR-HR-002 for reasons connected with a report), harassment, or threats. Anyone who experiences or observes retaliation should report it at once. Retaliation is gross misconduct under Section 11.4 of NXR-HR-001.

10.5 **Vigil mechanism.** This Section constitutes the Company's vigil mechanism. Concerns about the CEO, the CFO, or a member of the Ethics Committee may be raised directly with the Chair of the Audit Committee at auditchair@nexoratech.example.

10.6 **Regulator reporting.** Nothing in this Code stops anyone from reporting possible violations of law to a government agency or regulator.

## 11. Reporting Channels

11.1 A person who has a concern may choose any of the following channels:

| Channel | Best For |
|---|---|
| Reporting manager | Everyday concerns and questions |
| HRBP or people@nexoratech.example | People-related concerns, harassment, discrimination |
| Internal Committee (ic@nexoratech.example) | Sexual harassment complaints under the POSH Act |
| Ethics helpline (ethics@nexoratech.example) and 24x7 third-party hotline | Any breach of this Code, including anonymous reports |
| Head of Legal & Compliance | Legal, conflicts of interest, gifts, bribery |
| security@nexoratech.example | Security incidents and data breaches (within one hour) |
| DPO (dpo@nexoratech.example) | Privacy questions and data subject requests |
| Audit Committee Chair (auditchair@nexoratech.example) | Concerns about senior management |

11.2 Reports should contain as much detail as possible: who, what, when, where, and any evidence. Reporters do not need to be sure that a breach has occurred.

11.3 Managers who receive a report must not investigate it themselves. They must forward it to the HRBP, the Ethics Committee, or the Internal Committee, as appropriate, within 2 working days, and must protect the reporter's confidentiality.

## 12. Investigation Process

12.1 **Acknowledgement.** The Ethics Committee, or the Head of Legal & Compliance on its behalf, acknowledges each report within 3 working days.

12.2 **Assessment.** The Ethics Committee assesses each report and decides who will investigate. Matters involving sexual harassment are referred exclusively to the IC. Matters involving information security are investigated jointly with the CISO's team. Financial matters, including expense and travel fraud under NXR-FIN-001, are investigated jointly with the CFO's team, unless the CFO is involved.

12.3 **Fair process.** Investigations are impartial, thorough, and confidential. The person who is the subject of the allegation is told of it, given a fair chance to respond, and may be accompanied by a colleague at any interview, as provided in Section 11.3 of NXR-HR-001.

12.4 **Timelines.** The Ethics Committee aims to complete investigations within **45 working days** of the acknowledgement. Complex matters may take longer, and the reporter is told when this is the case. POSH inquiries follow the 90-day timeline in Section 5.5.

12.5 **Outcome.** The Ethics Committee decides on findings and recommends actions to the CHRO and the relevant Department Head. Where the findings involve possible criminal activity, the Head of Legal & Compliance will advise on referral to law enforcement.

12.6 **Records.** Records are kept securely by Legal & Compliance for at least 8 years and are accessed only by the Ethics Committee and other authorised persons.

12.7 **Feedback.** The reporter, where known, is told when the matter has been closed and, as far as confidentiality allows, of the outcome.

## 13. Consequences of Breach

13.1 A breach of this Code is a serious matter. Depending on the seriousness, the consequences may include counselling, a written warning, a final warning, withholding of a bonus or salary revision, suspension, demotion, or termination, as set out in Section 11 of NXR-HR-001. For contractors and vendors, the consequences include termination of the contract.

13.2 The following are examples of conduct that will normally be treated as gross misconduct, and may result in dismissal:

- Sexual harassment, violence, or threats.
- Bribery, corruption, fraud, or theft.
- Falsification of records, including attendance, expense claims, and travel claims, as provided in Section 13 of NXR-FIN-001.
- Serious breaches of information security, including unauthorised production access, credential sharing, and data exfiltration, as provided in Section 6.10 of NXR-IT-001.
- Deliberate breach of confidentiality or misuse of personal data.
- Retaliation against a person who reports in good faith.
- Failing to declare a material conflict of interest.

13.3 Where a breach also involves a violation of law, the Company may report it to the authorities and may seek recovery of losses.

13.4 Employees on probation who breach this Code may be dismissed on 15 days' notice, or without notice for gross misconduct, in accordance with Section 5.6 of NXR-HR-001. Such action must never be used as a form of retaliation as described in Section 10.4.
